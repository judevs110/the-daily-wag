Where We Walk

Windsor Routes

Six stunning routes across Windsor & Eton — chosen for every dog's pace, energy, and personality.

Windsor Great Park

The crown jewel of Windsor dog walking. Spanning over 4,800 acres, this royal park offers woodland trails, open meadows, and the iconic Savill Garden perimeter. Dogs can roam vast grassy stretches and explore ancient woodland paths all year round.

Our routes wind through the deer park (dogs kept on leads in deer areas), past Virginia Water lake, and into the quieter forest trails that most visitors never find. Perfect for high-energy dogs who need space to stretch their legs.

Off-lead areas Woodland trails Open meadows Best for high-energy breeds
Moderate 4.8 km one way 60 min

The Long Walk

One of Britain's most famous tree-lined avenues, stretching 4.8 km straight from Windsor Castle to the Copper Horse statue atop Snow Hill. The wide, flat gravel path offers stunning views of the castle and an almost magical sense of grandeur.

We typically walk the first half and loop back through the adjacent parkland, giving dogs a mix of open avenue and softer grass underfoot. Quiet enough in the early mornings to let calmer dogs walk at their own pace.

Flat terrain Iconic views All-weather path Great for all breeds
Easy 2–3 km 45–60 min

Eton & River Thames

Cross Windsor Bridge into the charming town of Eton and follow the Thames towpath for a serene riverside stroll. The flat, well-maintained path runs alongside the river, offering peaceful views of rowing crews, swans, and weeping willows.

This route is particularly popular for older dogs and puppies on their first walks — gentle underfoot, minimal traffic, and plenty of interesting smells along the riverbank. We often stop at the small riverside green for a rest.

Riverside path Gentle terrain Scenic towpath Ideal for seniors & puppies
Moderate 3–4 km 60 min

Old Windsor Meadows

A hidden gem tucked south of Windsor. Old Windsor's riverside meadows and Ham Island offer a completely different feel from the busier town routes — quieter, wilder, and wonderfully unspoilt. The meadows flood in winter, creating a rich habitat full of interesting scents for dogs.

Our circular route takes in the meadow footpaths, the Thames bank near Magna Carta Island, and the quieter lanes through the village. A brilliant choice for dogs who love sniffing through long grass and exploring off the beaten track.

Meadow footpaths Low foot traffic Riverside sections Great for sniffy dogs
Easy 1.5–2.5 km 30–45 min

Dedworth Green & Broom Farm

Our local neighbourhood route for dogs who live in or around Dedworth. Dedworth Green and the surrounding Broom Farm estate have well-kept parks, green corridors, and quiet residential paths that are perfect for shorter, lower-intensity walks.

This is the go-to route for puppy visits and mid-day toilet walks. Familiar and consistent — great for dogs who find new environments stressful, or those being eased back into exercise after illness or injury.

Quiet streets Local parks Short walk option Ideal for anxious dogs
Moderate 2.5–3.5 km 45–60 min

Clewer Village & Nature Reserve

One of Windsor's best-kept secrets. Clewer Village retains a genuine rural character despite being minutes from the town centre. Our route passes through the historic churchyard, along the mill stream, and into Clewer Nature Reserve — a wetland haven for wildlife.

The nature reserve is an SSSI (Site of Special Scientific Interest) with boardwalk paths over the wetland, reed beds, and open water. Dogs must be kept on leads here, but the rich smells and sounds keep even the most easily bored dogs fully engaged.

Nature reserve Wetland boardwalks Historic village lanes Rich wildlife scents
